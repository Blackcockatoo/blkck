Civic creativity project · Frankston, Victoria · The Old Vic State laboratory

Frankston 2035.

A ten-year civic creativity project for Frankston: permanent artwork in schools, healthy technology in the library, and a QR art walk through the CBD and foreshore. Three pilots are scoped, costed and documented. None have been built yet.

Frankston 2035 is the laboratory. Old Vic State is the statewide thesis it is testing.

The proposition

Murals become classrooms.
Libraries become studios.

Frankston is not short of people who can make things. It is short of the infrastructure that turns individual creative acts into civic culture — the systems, spaces and permissions. This project builds that infrastructure, one pilot at a time, over ten years.

Every pilot is designed to become permanent. Every artwork is designed to carry educational content. Every technology is designed without data extraction. Every program is designed to transfer ownership to the community.

Three proving grounds · none built yet

The pilots.

Three pilots, three settings, one purpose: prove the model works before scaling it. Scoped, costed and documented — and currently at proposal stage. Budget ranges are informed estimates with contingency, not supplier quotes.

Proposed

01 — The Living Mural

A curriculum-linked mural at a Frankston primary school, co-designed with students, painted by a professional artist and owned permanently by the school. Teachers receive a freely licensed resource pack.

12 months · $45,000–$65,000 indicative · school site to be identified with Council

Proposed

02 — The Healthy Tech Library

A creative technology commons in Frankston Library: drawing, music, writing and animation on offline, account-free, ad-free tools only. Includes a facilitator program and a Healthy Technology Charter the Library adopts. The recommended first pilot.

6 months setup · $30,000–$50,000 indicative · not yet scoped with the Library

Proposed

03 — The QR Art Walk

Ten to fifteen stops through the CBD and foreshore. Each QR code opens a local story, song, history or artwork. No app, no account, no tracking. The walk grows as new community stories are gathered, and belongs to the city.

6 months development · $20,000–$35,000 indicative · delivery mechanism already proven

Protocol

First Nations protocol.

Frankston is on Bunurong Country, in the Kulin Nation. Sovereignty was never ceded. Every mural wall, foreshore path, library room and QR plaque in this project sits on Country.

The project carries a nine-part written protocol covering research, intermediaries, first contact, listening, time, documentation and the difference between a meeting, a relationship, a partnership and a permission. It states plainly that Blue $nake Studio does not begin from an existing relationship, does not speak for any community, and does not treat Acknowledgement of Country as engagement.

Status: drafted, published, and not yet reviewed by a First Nations advisor. It must not be used operationally until that review has occurred. A review, a correction or a refusal are all valid responses and all wanted.

Honesty layer

The claims register.

Every major claim this project makes is written down, graded and published — including the weak ones. Two claims are fully proven. Eight require caveats or further evidence before they belong in a Council-facing document.

  • Proven — QR codes can create community walks with no app, account or tracking. The studio has deployed this repeatedly.
  • Proven — Blue $nake Studio has the component capabilities: public art, community materials, QR and web delivery, educational resource design, documentation, healthy-technology practice.
  • Draft — murals can teach; healthy tech works in a library; public art builds belonging; budgets are realistic; community appetite exists. Well supported in the literature, not yet measured in Frankston.
  • Proposal only — co-design strengthens students' connection to school; the model is replicable by other councils. Neither can be claimed until a pilot is complete.

What this is not

Frankston 2035 is not a Council plan, a policy claim or a funded program. It is an independent proposal from a Frankston studio, written so that a council, school or library can read it, argue with it and adopt the parts that are useful. No partnership, endorsement or funding relationship currently exists.

The wider argument

Frankston first. Not Frankston only.

Everything here is designed to be documented and handed on, so another Victorian town can do its own version rather than receive a copy of this one. That wider argument — keep what has weight, create what is missing, put art back into civic life, give young people ownership, and make every place unmistakably itself — is set out in full on the Old Vic State page.
